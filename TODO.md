# ASTRA Junior — Delivery Outcomes

## 1. Mission-control interface and progression
- [ ] Deliver a polished, responsive browser game named **ASTRA Junior — Become Mission-Ready** for the challenge “Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer”; it must feel like a premium futuristic mission-control simulator that is friendly, exciting, educational, easy for young learners to understand, and not like a generic dashboard or simple resource-management game.
- [ ] Use deep midnight navy/black-blue space gradients, translucent glass panels, electric cyan, soft violet, warm amber, and oxygen green, subtle grid/technical borders, atmospheric glow, readable high-contrast type, original identity, bundled typography, and no imitation of another game/franchise or NASA control-room screenshots.
- [ ] Provide top navigation with ASTRA JUNIOR/orbital mark, “MISSION CONTROL ONLINE” and live indicator, sound toggle, and astronaut initials/avatar.
- [ ] Provide a responsive training sidebar with “TRAINING PROGRAM,” “SPACE APPRENTICE,” circular rank progress, “2 of 10 missions complete” initial indication, ten levels, completed checkmarks, current-level cyan active line, locked-level markers, “How to play,” and “Reset training.” Each unlocked level can be selected; locked levels explain how to unlock them.
- [ ] Present mission label/title/description, Moon or Mars location chip, mission clock, level-specific objective, main risk, event, objective checklist, and plain-language Mission Control message.
- [ ] Use reusable level/action/resource/skill/badge/event data structures rather than ten separate page layouts.
- [ ] Include title/start, continue, how-to-play, profile/readiness, credits, briefing, outpost dashboard, decision, explain-your-decision, mission review/report, retry, and next-level interactions. The student should be able to start playing within thirty seconds.

## 2. Outpost scene and resource telemetry
- [ ] Build a non-placeholder, original procedural CSS/SVG/Three.js isometric outpost scene: lunar/Martian terrain, glowing horizon, large transparent habitat dome, smaller research dome, communication tower, solar-panel arrays, small rover, visible route, pulsing/rotating signal marker, subtle starfield/atmosphere, “HABITAT A,” “SOLAR ARRAY,” “ROVER 02” telemetry labels, zoom controls, and reset-view control.
- [ ] Keep the scene performant and usable on mobile; do not introduce unnecessary heavy 3D libraries or generated/branded art. Update scene cues when resources/events change.
- [ ] Show a legend for life support, communications, and low power reserve.
- [ ] Display five primary glass resource cards for Oxygen, Water, Power, Food, and Crew Health. Each has a distinctive icon, current percentage, small trend indicator, animated progress bar, and one-line plain-language text status such as Stable, Warning, or Critical; accessibility must not rely on color alone.
- [ ] Include Materials as the sixth core resource from the project guide without removing any of the five required primary cards; expose Communication, mission time, and science as secondary indicators where useful.
- [ ] Use learner-friendly bounded resource rules. No resource becomes negative or exceeds its documented cap without a clear reason; a single mistake does not end a mission. Provide Stable, Warning, and Critical states with a recovery opportunity before mission failure.

## 3. Ten level-specific training missions
- [ ] Level 1 — **Welcome to Astronaut Training**: teach oxygen, water, food, power, temperature, and communication; match six problems to outpost systems (including broken air filter, empty battery, water leak, failed heater); pass at least 4 of 6; explain errors and allow retry; badge **Systems Spotter**.
- [ ] Level 2 — **Choose Your Spacesuit**: choose among oxygen tank, radiation protection, thermal protection, communication device, tool kit, emergency battery, and sample container within a limited 5-slot/weight capacity for a short rock-sample EVA; provide safety/oxygen return conditions and the trade-off that extra tools add science potential but weight and reduce flexibility; badge **Mission Preparer**.
- [ ] Level 3 — **Build Your Mission Kit**: choose among food, water, batteries, repair tools, medical supplies, seeds, scientific instruments, and spare filters under both storage and mass limits; include an emergency reserve versus science/food-variety trade-off; badge **Resource Planner**.
- [ ] Level 4 — **Lunar Power Challenge**: manage long low sunlight with solar panels, batteries, heating, life support, lighting, communications, and laboratory; choose systems to keep active, whether to pause lab/reserve battery/reduce greenhouse lighting; maintain life support and communications until sunlight returns; badge **Power Guardian**.
- [ ] Level 5 — **Radiation and EVA Mission**: choose shielding, route, duration, tools/science sample, and return time; trigger a radiation warning; complete essential inspection within safety limit; explain added science versus added exposure; badge **Safety Specialist**.
- [ ] Level 6 — **Greenhouse and Water Mission**: manage lighting, water recycling, plant selection, temperature, food storage, and oxygen; fast-growing crops use more water, efficient crops grow slowly, oxygen-producing plants help air but yield less food; keep crew supplied without exhausting water; badge **Life-Support Farmer**.
- [ ] Level 7 — **Mars Communication Delay**: act before Mission Control replies; support water leak, damaged rover, power warning, medical concern, or sensor failure; make decisions with incomplete information and explain waiting for information versus preventing worsening harm; badge **Independent Decision-Maker**.
- [ ] Level 8 — **Dust Storm Emergency**: manage battery, experiments, greenhouse lighting, rover location, solar-panel repair/cleaning, and communications during a Martian dust storm; keep life support operating and show that not every system can be protected equally; badge **Storm Commander**.
- [ ] Level 9 — **Crew Health and Teamwork**: balance work, sleep, exercise, maintenance, science, and personal time; include fatigue, disagreement, medical concern, and experiment deadline events; allow task reassignment, delay science, rest, crew help, or robot; keep health safe while completing essential work; badge **Crew Leader**.
- [ ] Level 10 — **Mission Commander Challenge**: combine oxygen, water, food, power, materials, radiation safety, communication delay, crew health, science, and repairs. Scenario: dust storm approaching, greenhouse needs power, astronaut outside repairing a solar panel, water recycler stopped, Mission Control cannot respond immediately. Player prioritizes actions, assigns crew, manages resources, and explains the key decision. Score safe outcomes and reasoning even if science is lost; badge **Junior Mission Commander**.
- [ ] Show different objectives and visual context for every level; selecting an available level updates title, location, objective, event, choices, systems, badge, and scene context.

## 4. Decisions, consequences, explanation, and learning feedback
- [ ] Decision section is titled “What will you prioritize?” and contains interactive choices such as Keep life support online, Save power for the storm, Pause the laboratory, Reduce greenhouse lighting, and Send the rover to inspect the array.
- [ ] Each decision card shows its title, simple explanation, immediate benefit, immediate cost, delayed-risk label, selection/confirmation state, and visible keyboard focus. Decisions can be made by keyboard and pointer.
- [ ] Selecting a decision animates the card, updates bounded resource values and resource trends, changes relevant scene/status cues, records time/action/consequence in chronological Mission Log, triggers relevant event/delayed consequence, updates astronaut skill scores, shows clear feedback and a cause-and-effect chain, and asks the player to explain the choice.
- [ ] Every important action has at least one benefit, at least one cost, and a possible delayed consequence. Do not reveal every future consequence in advance, but explain it fairly after the choice.
- [ ] Offer younger learners selectable reasoning statements and allow older learners a short written explanation. Reward explanations that identify priorities, risks, and consequences, not only the outcome.
- [ ] Display recent mission-log entries with time, decision, immediate consequence, and status icon; provide functioning Clear and/or View all control.
- [ ] The level loop is Briefing → Learn → Prepare → Act → Consequence → Explain → Review → Advance; feedback explains what improved, what worsened, and why. Failure is educational and recoverable; allow retry.

## 5. Readiness, mission memory, and report
- [ ] Show a readiness profile/skill constellation or radar for Safety awareness, Resource management, Planning, Science/scientific thinking, Emergency response, Teamwork, Sustainability, and Decision explanation.
- [ ] Show a numerical readiness score while explaining that it reflects reasoning, planning, and safe teamwork as well as survival; avoid an opaque “mystery points” formula.
- [ ] Provide transparent report categories: Safety, Resource Management, Emergency Response, Teamwork, Sustainability, Decision Explanation, plus the wider readiness skill profile and feedback; internally use the project guide's suggested weights if a composite is shown (safety .25, resources .20, emergency response .15, teamwork .15, sustainability .10, science .10, explanation .05).
- [ ] “OPEN MISSION REPORT” opens an accessible glassmorphism dialog with mission outcome/duration, safety/resource/emergency/teamwork/sustainability/decision-explanation scores, best decision, most dangerous decision, systems protected/neglected, strongest skill, recommended next training, badge, and personalized title such as “The Careful Systems Thinker.”
- [ ] Include the exact callout: “You protected the systems that keep the crew alive. Next, practice balancing safety with science.” Provide functioning Return to mission, Retry level, and Continue training buttons.
- [ ] Mission Memory summarizes the player's decisions, resources at start/end, cause-and-effect chain, skill feedback, and next learning goal; reward safe reasoning even if optional science progress is lower.

## 6. Accessibility, controls, and persistence
- [ ] Use semantic header/nav/main/section/article/button/dialog elements and useful ARIA labels; support keyboard navigation, visible focus, readable contrast, and screen-reader-friendly labels. Provide textual explanations for icons and never communicate a state using color alone.
- [ ] Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; provide a low-motion setting; avoid distracting motion/flashing/warnings. Include sound toggle and make all content/gameplay usable muted; audio must not be required and any audio has text/subtitle equivalent.
- [ ] Support desktop, tablet, and mobile layouts; touch targets, dialogue/actions, telemetry and outpost controls remain usable at narrow widths.
- [ ] Save and restore mission progress, decisions/log, resources, skills, badges, current/unlocked/completed level and relevant report data in browser local storage under `astraJuniorProgress`; preserve audio/display preferences in the starter save; safely recover malformed/missing saves.
- [ ] “Reset training” works and asks for confirmation before clearing training progress; reset should not silently clear unrelated browser data. Continue resumes a saved mission, Retry resets the current level, and Continue training advances/unlocks appropriately.

## 7. Technical quality, credits, and delivery
- [ ] Adapt the initialized Three.js/TypeScript/Vite project in place and preserve its engine/runtime, settings save, keyboard/input/audio mechanisms where applicable, Tweak manager/bridge, dependency lock, and established project/build identity. Keep player-facing game content in English and do not auto-switch based on browser locale.
- [ ] Keep page fast, use relative asset paths, and work in the browser without installation, server, login, database, or online multiplayer. No external branded art or unrequested generative game assets.
- [ ] No fake buttons or placeholder scene. Every visible actionable control responds to interaction; any locked item clearly explains its progression requirement.
- [ ] Include README setup and run instructions, educational-purpose explanation, credits/licensing, bundled font/library attribution, original art attribution, and AI-assistance disclosure guidance if required by the event.
- [ ] Preserve original asset/license notices and open-source library credits; use bundled CC0 fonts and Three.js/Rapier notices if their code remains in use. Include source reference statement from the supplied guide: 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge problem description supplied in the conversation, and user-supplied ASTRA Junior plan; do not present as NASA endorsement.
- [ ] Run the existing build/test/smoke workflows as applicable; validate mission rules and progression using existing checks plus focused rules tests. Fix actionable diagnostics. Before the first complete checkpoint, create a game-specific favicon, prepare sharing/build metadata as required, then save the exact intended source through the canonical project checkpoint process. Do not publish unless separately authorized.
